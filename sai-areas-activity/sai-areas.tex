\begin{center}
  \textbf{ Math 202 -- Slice, approximate, integrate: the area between two curves
  --- Fall 2024
    }
\end{center}

For each of the regions listed below:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Draw a graph of all the relevant curves. (Using Desmos or something is a good choice.)
    \item Find the intersection points of the curves. (Using Desmos or something is a good choice.)
    \item Shade in the region that's ``bounded by'' these curves.
    \item Draw a representative slice. Decide whether the slice should be vertical or horizontal.
    \item Label the width of the slice and the height of the slice.
    \item Use your labels to write an equation for $\Delta A$.
\end{itemize}
Notice that ``write down an integral'' is not part of these directions!
\vfill

Here's the regions:

\begin{enumerate}[a.]
\item The finite region bounded by $y=12-2x^2$ and $y=x^2-8$.
\item The finite region bounded by $x=y^2$ and $x=6-2y^2$.
\item The finite region bounded by $x=y^2$ and $x=4y$.
\item The finite region bounded by $x=1-y^2$ and $x=2-2y^2$.
\item The finite regions between the curves $y=x^3-x$ and $y=x^2$.
\item The area bounded by the $x$-axis, $y=x^2$, and $y=2-x$.
\item The area bounded by the $y$-axis, $y=x^2$, and $y=2-x$.
\item The area bounded by the $y$-axis, $f(x)=\cos(x)$, and $g(x)=\sin(x)$. \\(How far do you figure we should go?)
\item The finite region between the curves $x=y^2-2y$ and $y=x$.
\end{enumerate}
